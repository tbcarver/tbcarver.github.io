CreaStix market and sales analysis

TruLife Naturals · prepared 18 November 2026 · data through 17 November 2026

Executive summary

CreaStix is selling poorly because almost no qualified buyer has seen it, and the few who do get little proof to trust an unknown brand and an unfamiliar format. The creatine market is strong and the website is not broken.

  • The market is growing fast, but not for sticks. US creatine sales grew about 72% in retail and 90% on Amazon. Shoppers search for gummies (74,000 a month), not sticks (260) or "direct to mouth" (0).
  • The site has sold 12 pouches to 9 customers in 11 weeks. 8 of those 12 orders came through three or four affiliate creators. No stranger has found CreaStix through search and bought it.
  • Traffic is tiny and mostly junk. About 46% of 1,808 sessions are invalid or suspicious. Only about 177 real, non-affiliate visitors saw a CreaStix page in 87 days.
  • Price is a problem on Amazon. $31.99 with no discount sits next to stick packs at $19 to $25, and the ads were promoting the flavor with zero ratings. On the site, 9 of 12 orders used a discount.
  • One $30 pouch cannot pay for ads. Buying a supplement customer on Meta costs $46 to $80 in published benchmarks.
  • The plan is a sequence: stop wasted spend, earn proof (reviews, lab certificate), fix the offer and price (discount, trial pack, bundle), then buy attention through creators and samples. Paid ads come last.

1. The market: booming, but shoppers want gummies

Creatine is one of the fastest-growing supplements in the US. Growth is coming from women and older adults, not gym regulars. The stick format has almost no search demand of its own, so CreaStix has to borrow demand from another format.

Monthly US Google searches

Google Keyword Planner, November 2026. Log scale. "Direct to mouth creatine" returns 0.

Year-over-year sales growth

Retail: SPINS, 52 weeks to 30 Nov 2025. Amazon: SmartScout, 12 months to Aug 2026 ($721M).

  • Who is new to creatine: women were 30% of GNC creatine purchases, up from 18% in 2020. In one 2026 survey, 82.5% of people who never used creatine said they were interested.
  • Direct competitor: one confirmed US pour-in-mouth rival, EHP Labs Creatine Fizz Stix, at $1.87 a stick. Its Amazon listing shows 26 ratings and "400+ bought in past month".
  • Big brands are entering sticks: Optimum Nutrition, AG1, Orgain and Cellucor all launched creatine sticks in 2026, though theirs mix into water.
  • Gummy weakness: independent tests found many creatine gummies contain far less creatine than labeled. A stick with a published lab result can use that.

2. Price and positioning: cheap against gummies, expensive against tubs

The current pitch, "no water, no scoop", speaks to people who already use tubs. For them CreaStix costs two to seven times as much per dose. Against gummies it is cheaper and delivers a full 5 g.

What this means

  • Target the gummy buyer: lead with taste, then the verified full 5 g dose, then the price against gummies.
  • Raise the first order: a $59 to $79 bundle and a low-price three-flavor trial pack. Every CreaStix order so far is a single pouch.
  • Soften "no water": it has no search demand and invites the dry-scoop safety objection. Say "no mixing" and allow a sip of water.
  • Proof is missing: "third-party tested" has no named lab or certificate, and site reviews are typed-in text with no source.

3. The website: fine at converting, starved of real visitors

Figures are from Google Analytics for 23 August to 17 November 2026 (87 days). The filtered traffic converts at about 2%, which is a normal rate for a health store. There are simply too few real visitors.

Site funnel, 18 Sep to 17 Nov

Users who saw the homepage, landing page or product page, then added to cart, then bought.

  • Affiliate links: 574 sessions, 32% of all traffic, averaging 2.4 seconds each. The largest single affiliate sent 211 sessions and no orders.
  • Unexplained burst: 209 iPhone sessions on 4 to 8 November with almost no engagement and no known source.
  • Page problems verified: subscription is preselected on the product page; the 10% offer is a one-time 10-minute countdown; price and buy button sit below the first screen on mobile; no Shop Pay or Apple Pay button before checkout; no email capture offer.
  • What works: pages load fast (about 2.0 seconds on a throttled phone) and tracking records purchases correctly.

4. Who actually bought: 12 orders, mostly through creators

Figures are from Shopify order records, 1 September to 17 November 2026. Google Analytics counted 9 of these; it missed two subscription renewals and one Shop app order.

  • Creators are the only working channel: 44 creator discount codes were issued and 3 have ever been used. One creator's code brought 3 CreaStix orders plus an order for other products.
  • No cold search buyers: the brand's other products do sell to strangers from search (8 of their 10 paid orders, at full price). CreaStix does not yet.
  • Narrow geography: 9 of 12 orders shipped to Utah and Montana.
  • One repeat signal: a subscriber is on a third monthly shipment. No refunds.
  • Discounts: 9 of 12 orders were discounted; the average paid was about $28. The countdown offer code has been used once in seven weeks.

5. Amazon: expensive clicks landing on the weakest flavor

Observed on the live listing on 18 November 2026. The actual ad spend figures were not available, so the ad arithmetic uses published benchmarks.

Price per serving against stick competitors

Ad cost as a share of sales

At a $2.27 click and a $31.99 sale, by conversion rate. Break-even is roughly 30 to 55%, depending on product cost.

  • No visible deal: no coupon or strike-through price, while stick competitors show 13 to 20% discounts.
  • Listing gaps: no pack count in any title, two flavors with 4 images against 8 on the third, no brand video, and no testing claim.
  • The stick niche is open: the median page-one stick listing has about 270 ratings, against 20,000 to 85,000 for tubs on the main creatine terms.
  • A benchmark to copy: one stick brand launched in late 2025 sells 3,000+ a month with only 190 ratings, at $24.99 with a strike-through price.
  • Unused asset: the brand's Trace Mineral Complex has 156 ratings and 500+ monthly sales, and could anchor a bundle.

7. Limits of this analysis

  • Small numbers: 12 orders are too few to say which page fix matters most, or to separate price resistance from lack of trust.
  • Missing data: Amazon ad spend, clicks and orders were not available, and the product's landed cost is unknown, so break-even figures are illustrative.
  • Benchmarks: most channel cost and conversion benchmarks are published by vendors with a commercial interest.
  • Snapshots: Amazon prices, ratings and ad placements were observed once, on 18 November 2026.
Sources: Shopify order records, Google Analytics 4, Google Keyword Planner, live Amazon listings, SPINS and SmartScout figures as cited in trade press, and published benchmark reports. Creator names and codes are omitted from this page.
